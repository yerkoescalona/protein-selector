**Important note for students:** Always confirm the ligand code in bold in column 7 before
selecting a residue in your viewer — several of these crystals contain additional HETATM
entries (ions like ZN/CL/NA/CA/K, crystallization additives like SO4/GOL/BME/IPA, or
cofactors like HEM) that are *not* the validated ligand and are excluded from this table's
candidate set; do not confuse them with the real ligand. Two rows have genuine multi-ligand
ambiguity resolved here explicitly by Meeko-parameterization pass/fail and pipeline "largest
organic ligand" logic — row 20 (3DNA: use PIH, not HED, which failed parameterization) and
rows 14/22 (2OMG: use CRS, not URE; 2B5S: use DAO, not HP6 — both alternates passed
parameterization but were smaller, so the pipeline did not select them). For the five
HIV-1 protease rows (24–28) and the two T4-lysozyme-family triplicates (18–20, 8–9), the
ligand — not the protein chain — is the meaningful identifier: always dock/select by the
bolded CCD code, not by assuming "chain A" is where the ligand sits (row 24's THK and rows
25–28's ligands sit on chain B or A inconsistently; row 22's/14's ligand appears on multiple
chains). Row 6 (1A7E) is a documented PLIP-on-metal-cluster limitation, not a "no binding"
result — treat it as a toolchain caveat, not a teaching example of a weak ligand.
